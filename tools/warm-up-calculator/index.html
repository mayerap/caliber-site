How the warm-up is built

Warm-ups climb in percentage of your working weight while reps fall. For heavy sets of 5 or fewer, the ramp is the bar for 10, then about 40%, 60%, 75% and 85% of your working weight. Sets of 6 to 8 use a shorter ramp, and higher-rep work needs only a couple of sets. Every weight rounds to 5 lb (or 2.5 kg) steps, and duplicate or too-heavy sets are dropped.

Tips

  • Move each warm-up with intent. Treat them as practice reps, not filler.
  • Add a set on cold or stiff days. An extra light set costs almost nothing.
  • Don’t warm up to fatigue. If warm-ups feel hard, cut the reps, not the sets.

Frequently asked questions

How many warm-up sets do I need?

Heavier work needs more. For sets of 5 or fewer, four or five warm-up sets is common. For sets of 8 or more, two or three is usually enough.

Why do the reps go down as the weight goes up?

Warm-ups prepare you without tiring you. Fewer reps at heavier weights let you feel the load and groove technique while saving energy for the work sets.

Should I warm up every exercise?

Warm up the first heavy lift fully. Later exercises that use the same muscles often need only one or two lighter sets.

Does this work in kilograms?

Yes. Choose kg, and loads round to 2.5 kg steps with a 20 kg bar by default.
