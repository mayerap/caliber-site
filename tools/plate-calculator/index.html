How it works

Most plate calculators assume a full commercial gym and load the biggest plates first. That works when every plate size is on the rack. Home and garage sets are usually incomplete, and biggest-first loading can miss weights you can actually build.

This calculator lists every load your plates can make, then picks the one closest to your target, using the fewest plates. It also shows the closest weight above your target, and which pair of plates would add the most new loads if you buy one.

An example

Say you own a 45 lb bar, one pair of 45s and two pairs of 25s. For 145 lb you need 50 lb per side. Biggest-first loading grabs a 45, has nothing small enough to finish, and leaves you at 135. Two 25s per side make 145 exactly. The calculator finds combinations like that for you.

Tips for a home plate set

  • Small plates matter most for progress. A pair of 2.5 lb plates gives you 5 lb jumps. Without them, every jump is at least 10 lb, which stalls presses quickly.
  • Pairs of 10s are flexible. Two pairs of 10s and one pair of 5s cover every 10 lb step from the bar to 50 lb per side.
  • When you can’t load a weight, round down and add a rep, rather than rounding up and missing reps.

Plate math on every set, in Caliber

Caliber shows the plates for every set as you train. Save the plates you own at home and Caliber uses only those while you train there, rounding each load to what you can build and adding reps to keep the effort the same.

Frequently asked questions

Why can’t I just load the biggest plates first?

With a full gym set, biggest-first works. With a limited home set it can miss. If you own one pair of 45s and two pairs of 25s, biggest-first loading for 145 lb puts a 45 on each side and comes up 10 lb short, while two 25s per side make it exactly. This calculator checks every combination you own.

Does the calculator include collars?

No. Spring collars weigh almost nothing. Competition collars weigh 2.5 kg each; if you use them, add their weight to the bar weight.

What does “best next pair to buy” mean?

For each common plate size, the calculator adds one more pair to your set and counts how many new loads that unlocks, up to 100 lb (or 50 kg) beyond your current maximum. The pair that unlocks the most is suggested.

Is my plate set saved?

Yes, on this device only, in your browser’s storage. Nothing is sent anywhere.
