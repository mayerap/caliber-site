How it works

The calculator adds your reps in reserve to the reps you did, then uses the Epley formula to estimate a one-rep max: weight × (1 + total reps ÷ 30). It reverses the same formula for your target reps and effort, then rounds to 5 lb or 2.5 kg.

Estimates are most reliable for sets of about 10 reps or fewer. On long sets, people tend to have a little more in reserve than they think, as a 2022 review found.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is an estimated max?

It is an estimate. Formulas like Epley are most reliable for sets of about 10 reps or fewer, and your RPE rating adds its own error of about a rep. Use it to choose training weights, not to declare a new max.

Why does the calculator add reps in reserve?

A set of 5 at RPE 8 means you could have done about 7. Adding the reps in reserve gives a better estimate than treating every set as a max effort.

Should I use RPE or RIR?

Either. They convert directly: RPE = 10 − RIR. Use whichever your program is written in.

What target RPE should I use?

Most working sets sit between RPE 7 and 9. Heavier, lower-rep work usually sits at the higher end.
