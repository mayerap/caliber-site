The Texas Method, explained

The Texas Method is a three-day strength program with a hard volume day, a light recovery day, and a Friday where you try to set a new five-rep record. It is a common first step after a beginner linear program.

What the Texas Method is

The Texas Method is a three-day strength program for lifters who can no longer add weight every session. It is credited to weightlifting coach Glenn Pendlay and was popularized by Mark Rippetoe and Andy Baker in Practical Programming for Strength Training. Its idea is simple: one hard volume day, one easy day, and one day to set a new personal record.

The week

DayPurposeMain work
MondayVolumeSquat 5×5, bench or press 5×5, deadlift 1×5, all at about 90% of Friday’s weights
WednesdayRecoveryLight squats (about 80% of Monday) for 2×5, the other press, and back work like chin-ups
FridayIntensityWork up to a new 5-rep record on squat and the week’s press; often a power clean or other pull

Bench and overhead press usually alternate: one week bench is the Monday and Friday press, the next week overhead press takes that spot. Deadlifts stay low in volume because heavy squatting already trains the same muscles.

How progression works

Friday drives everything. Each week, aim to beat last Friday’s five-rep set by a small amount: 5 lb on squat, 2.5 to 5 lb on presses. Monday’s weights follow, staying around 90% of Friday. Wednesday stays light on purpose, so you arrive at Friday fresh.

The Texas Method works because of the recovery day. If Wednesday creeps heavy, Friday stops improving within a few weeks.

Starting it

  • Use your last good five-rep sets from your beginner program as the first Friday weights.
  • Set Monday at about 90% of those.
  • Expect Monday’s 5×5 to be the hardest session of the week. That is normal.

A sample week

For a lifter whose last Friday squat was 275 × 5:

DaySquatPress
Monday245 × 5 × 5 (about 90%)Bench 5 × 5 at about 90% of Friday’s bench
Wednesday195 × 5 × 2 (about 80% of Monday)Overhead press 3 × 5, moderate
FridayWork up to 280 × 5, a new recordBench, work up to a new 5-rep record

Next week, overhead press takes the Monday and Friday spot and bench moves to Wednesday.

Who it suits

The Texas Method suits lifters who can train three days a week, who just stopped making session-to-session progress, and who like a clear weekly target. It is less suited to people who need lots of upper-body volume for size, or who can’t control their sleep and stress, because weekly records depend on recovery.

Running it at home

All the main lifts need only a barbell, rack and bench. Chin-ups need a bar. Power cleans need bumper plates and a floor you can drop on; if you don’t have them, a heavy row or back extensions are reasonable substitutes on Friday.

Common mistakes

  1. Monday too heavy. If Monday leaves you wrecked until Friday, lower it to 85% to 88%.
  2. Wednesday too hard. Treat it as practice, not training.
  3. Big Friday jumps. Small jumps keep the program running for months. Big ones end it in weeks.
  4. Ignoring sleep and food. Weekly records need recovery. This program punishes a short night more than most.

When it stops working

Most lifters run the Texas Method for a few months before weekly records stop coming. Common adjustments are rotating Friday between fives, triples and doubles, or moving to a program with planned blocks. See what to run after your program ends.

Frequently asked questions

Who created the Texas Method?

It is credited to weightlifting coach Glenn Pendlay and was popularized by Mark Rippetoe and Andy Baker’s book Practical Programming for Strength Training.

How do I set Monday’s weight?

Start around 90% of the weight you can lift for a hard set of 5. As Friday’s five-rep record rises, Monday rises with it.

What if I stall on Friday?

First check recovery: sleep, food, and whether Monday is too heavy. Then try lowering Monday’s volume slightly, or switching Friday to triples or doubles for a few weeks.

Can I run the Texas Method for size?

It is mainly a strength program. Add a few sets of upper back, arm and leg accessories if size matters to you, but keep them modest so Friday stays strong.
