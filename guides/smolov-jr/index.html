Smolov Jr, explained

Smolov Jr is a three-week specialization block for one lift, usually squat or bench, with four sessions a week. It is short and very demanding, so use it occasionally and keep other training light while you run it.

What Smolov Jr is

Smolov Jr is a three-week specialization block for one lift, most often the squat or bench press. It is a shortened version of the much longer Russian Smolov squat program. It is hard, high-frequency and short, and it is designed to push one lift up quickly.

This is an advanced, high-fatigue block. Run it only if you are healthy, your technique is solid, and you can keep other training light for three weeks.

The week

Four sessions per week on the same lift, using percentages of your current one-rep max:

DaySets × repsWeight (week 1)
Day 16 × 670%
Day 27 × 575%
Day 38 × 480%
Day 410 × 385%

A common layout is Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Saturday. Some lifters spread the sessions differently to fit their week; keep at least one rest day before Day 1 of the next week.

Weeks 2 and 3

Weeks 2 and 3 use the same sets and reps. Instead of new percentages, you add a fixed amount to every week 1 weight: commonly 5 to 10 lb in week 2, and 10 to 20 lb in week 3 (about 2.5 to 5 kg, then 5 to 10 kg).

After the block

Take several easy days, then test or work up to a heavy single. Expect to feel flat in the first days after the block. Then return to your normal program with an updated max.

Sample weights

For a 315 lb squat max, week 1 rounds to:

DayWeek 1Week 2 (+10 lb)Week 3 (+20 lb)
6 × 6220 lb230 lb240 lb
7 × 5235 lb245 lb255 lb
8 × 4250 lb260 lb270 lb
10 × 3270 lb280 lb290 lb

A lifter at this strength might choose smaller additions, such as +5 lb and +15 lb, if week 1 feels hard.

Who it suits, and who should skip it

Smolov Jr suits experienced lifters with good technique, healthy joints and a specific reason to push one lift for a short time. Skip it if you are newer to lifting, recovering from an injury, cutting weight, or have a meet within about six weeks. A normal program progresses more safely in those situations.

Recovering through the block

Sleep and food matter more than usual. Keep other training to light upper-body work if you are running it for squats. Warm up thoroughly; the volume adds up quickly.

Common mistakes

  1. Using an inflated max. Every percentage comes from it. Use a recent, honest number.
  2. Big weekly jumps. The lower end of the range is enough for most lifters.
  3. Running other hard training alongside it. Smolov Jr takes most of your recovery.
  4. Grinding through pain. Joint pain is a reason to stop the block, not push harder.

Frequently asked questions

How much weight should I add in weeks 2 and 3?

Commonly 5 to 10 lb (about 2.5 to 5 kg) over week 1 in week 2, and 10 to 20 lb (about 5 to 10 kg) over week 1 in week 3. Stronger lifters use the higher end; lighter lifters and bench press use the lower end.

Can I run Smolov Jr for bench?

Yes. Many people run it for bench press with smaller weekly increases. Don’t run it for squat and bench at the same time.

What else can I train during Smolov Jr?

Very little. Keep other lifts light and short, and skip extra leg work if you run it for squats.

How often can I run it?

Treat it as an occasional specialization block, a few times a year at most, with normal training in between.
