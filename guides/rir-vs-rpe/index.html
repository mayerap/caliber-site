RIR vs RPE: what they mean and how to use them

RIR is how many more reps you could have done. RPE is a 1 to 10 effort rating, and on the scale lifters use, RPE equals 10 minus RIR. They measure the same thing, so pick whichever you find easier and use it consistently.

The definitions

RIR stands for reps in reserve: how many more reps you could have done before failing. Stop a set with 2 good reps left and that set was 2 RIR.

RPE stands for rating of perceived exertion. In lifting, it is a 1 to 10 scale where 10 is a maximum effort. The version most lifters use ties each number to reps in reserve, so the two scales measure the same thing in different ways.

RIR to RPE conversion chart

100Maximum effort. No more reps, no more weight
9.50No more reps, but slightly more weight was possible
91One more rep was possible
8.51 to 2Definitely one more, maybe two
82Two more reps were possible
7.52 to 3Two more for sure, maybe three
73Fast, controlled, three more possible
5 to 64 to 6Warm-up or technique work

The rule of thumb: RPE = 10 − RIR.

Where the scales came from

RPE started as a general exercise scale. Powerlifting coach Mike Tuchscherer of Reactive Training Systems popularized a lifting version tied to reps left in the tank. Researchers then formalized it as the RIR-based RPE scale, described by Zourdos and colleagues in 2016, with practical guidance from Helms and colleagues the same year.

How accurate are people?

Better than you might expect. A 2022 meta-analysis by Halperin and colleagues pooled 12 studies and found people underestimated their remaining reps by about one rep on average. Two findings are useful in practice:

  • Estimates got slightly more accurate closer to failure.
  • Estimates were much more accurate on sets of 12 reps or fewer than on longer sets.

So treat your RIR as a good estimate, not an exact count. On long sets, you probably have a bit more in the tank than you think. Taking an occasional last set to failure, on a safe exercise, recalibrates your sense of effort.

Which should you use?

  • Use RIR if you are newer to effort-based training, or you want a quick answer between sets. Counting reps left is intuitive.
  • Use RPE if your program is written in RPE, you compete in powerlifting, or you want half-point precision on heavy sets.

Since they convert directly, the choice is about habit. Pick one and use it consistently, so your logs are comparable over time.

Common mistakes

  1. Rating the whole session, not the set. Rate each set right after you finish it, while the effort is fresh.
  2. Sandbagging. Calling every set RPE 8 so the weights never go up. If your “RPE 8” bar speed never changes, test it with an honest extra rep now and then.
  3. Chasing RPE 10. Most work should sit between RPE 7 and 9. Grinding to failure on heavy compound lifts adds fatigue faster than it adds strength.
  4. Mixing scales. Logging RIR one week and RPE the next makes your history hard to read. Pick one.
  5. Ignoring bar speed. A rep that slows sharply is a sign you are close to your limit, even if it felt fine.

How to use them in a program

Effort targets make a program adjust to you. Instead of “5 reps at 225 lb,” a program says “5 reps at RPE 8.” On a good day that might be 235. On a bad day, 215. Over weeks, you train at the right effort far more often than with fixed weights.

  1. Start conservative. Choose a weight you think is RPE 7.
  2. Rate honestly after each set. If the target is RPE 8 and the set felt like RPE 6, add weight.
  3. Adjust in small steps. Roughly 2% to 4% per point of RPE is a reasonable change on main lifts.
  4. Log it. Your effort ratings are the data that make next week’s weights accurate.

Frequently asked questions

Is RPE 8 the same as 2 RIR?

Yes. On the RIR-based RPE scale used in strength training, RPE 8 means you could have done about 2 more reps. RPE = 10 minus RIR.

Which is better for beginners, RIR or RPE?

RIR. “How many more reps could I have done?” is easier to answer than a 1 to 10 rating. The numbers convert directly, so you can switch later.

How do I log a set where I missed a rep?

Log it as 0 RIR, or RPE 10, and note the missed rep. Then lower the weight or the reps on the next set.

Should I use RPE on accessories?

You can, but precision matters less there. On high-rep accessories, aim for about 0 to 2 reps in reserve and don’t overthink it.

How accurate are people at judging reps in reserve?

Reasonably accurate. A 2022 meta-analysis found people were off by about one rep on average, usually guessing they had fewer left than they did. Accuracy was better on sets of 12 reps or fewer and closer to failure.

What does RPE 9.5 mean?

You probably could not have done another rep, but you could have used slightly more weight. Half points let you rate sets between whole reps.
