What nSuns 5/3/1 is

nSuns 5/3/1 LP is a free powerlifting program written by Reddit user u/nSuns around 2016. It borrows the main-lift wave from Jim Wendler’s 5/3/1 and adds a high-volume block of backoff sets for a second lift. The result is a lot of practice on the squat, bench, deadlift and overhead press, with weight added every week.

The program spread through r/Fitness and r/weightroom and now has its own subreddit, where the original spreadsheets and the creator’s notes still live. If you run nSuns, read the original post once. This guide explains the structure in our own words so you can run it well, in a spreadsheet or in an app.

How the program is structured

Every training day has two barbell lifts and then accessories:

  • The first lift (often called T1) is the day’s main lift. You do 9 sets that ramp up to a heavy single-rep set, then work back down. The heavy set is marked “1+”, meaning do at least one rep and as many as you can with good form.
  • The second lift (T2) is a related lift, done for 8 lighter sets of moderate reps. On a bench day, for example, the second lift is overhead press.
  • Accessories are your choice. Most lifters add 2 to 4 exercises for upper back, arms, abs or weak points.

All main-lift weights are percentages of a training max, not your true max. The training max is usually 90% of your one-rep max. That buffer is what lets you hit so many sets and still add weight each week.

How progression works

The 1+ set drives everything. After each session, look at how many reps you got on it and adjust that lift’s training max for next week. The commonly used rules from the original program are:

Reps on the 1+ setChange to the training max
0 to 1No change
2 to 3Add 5 lb (about 2.5 kg)
4 to 5Add 5 to 10 lb
More than 5Add 10 to 15 lb

Lower-body lifts usually take the larger jumps and upper-body lifts the smaller ones. Check the original spreadsheet for the exact rules of the version you run, because some variants differ.

If you fail the 1+ set entirely two weeks in a row, the training max is too high. Drop it by about 5% to 10% and build back up. That is not a step back. It is how the program is meant to work.

Common mistakes

  1. Setting the training max from a true max. Use 90% of a recent, honest one-rep max, or of an estimate from a recent rep set. Starting heavy is the fastest way to stall in week three.
  2. Grinding every set. Only the 1+ set should be hard. The rest should move with good speed. If the backoff sets look like max attempts, the training max is too high.
  3. Skipping accessories, or doing too many. Upper back work keeps your shoulders healthy under all that pressing. But nSuns is already high volume, so 2 to 4 accessories is plenty.
  4. Running it forever. Weekly jumps eventually stop. Chasing them with tiny increases and repeated resets wastes months.

How to adjust it

  • Short on time: trim the T2 sets before you trim the main lift. The main lift and the 1+ set matter most.
  • Beat up: take a lighter week every 4 to 6 weeks. Do the same sets at roughly 70% of the usual weights, with no 1+ set.
  • Stalled on one lift: reset only that lift’s training max. The others can keep climbing.

Spreadsheet or app?

The original spreadsheets work. They also mean scrolling a tiny grid between sets, doing plate math in your head, and updating training maxes by hand. A dedicated app handles the math and timing for you. Several apps include nSuns as a built-in template. The other route, and the one Caliber takes, is to bring the spreadsheet you already use, so any changes you made to it come with you.

What to run after nSuns

When the 1+ sets stop moving, it is time for a program that plans variation for you: blocks that move from volume to heavier weights and end with a lighter week. That structure lets you keep progressing after weekly jumps run out. Our guide to what to run after your program ends covers the options.

Frequently asked questions

Is nSuns good for beginners?

Not usually. Despite the “LP” in the name, it carries a lot of volume: around 17 sets of main lifts per session before accessories. Most lifters do better running a simpler beginner program first, such as GZCLP, until adding weight every session stops working.

How long should I run nSuns?

Treat it as a phase. Many lifters run it for roughly three to five months, until the AMRAP sets stop climbing and the weekly jumps shrink to zero. Then move to a program with planned blocks rather than restarting nSuns from scratch.

Should I run the 4-day or 5-day version?

Start with 4 days. The 5-day version adds an extra bench and overhead-press day, which helps if your upper body lags, but it also adds fatigue. Move up only if you are recovering well on 4 days.

Can I run nSuns while cutting?

You can, but expect the AMRAP sets and the weekly increases to slow. A calorie deficit reduces how much volume you can recover from. If you cut, lower your training max and accept smaller jumps.

What should I run after nSuns?

Something with planned variation in volume and intensity, such as 5/3/1 Boring But Big or a block-periodized program. See our guide to what to run after your program ends.
