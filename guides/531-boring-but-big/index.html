5/3/1 Boring But Big, explained

5/3/1 Boring But Big pairs Jim Wendler’s three-set main-lift wave with five sets of ten for size. Progress comes from small training max increases each cycle, which keeps it working for a long time.

What 5/3/1 Boring But Big is

5/3/1 is a strength program by powerlifter and coach Jim Wendler. Its core idea is slow, steady progress on four lifts: squat, bench press, deadlift and overhead press. Boring But Big (BBB) is one of its best-known templates. It adds five sets of ten after the main lift for size and work capacity.

This guide explains the structure in our own words. Wendler’s books are the source and are worth buying if you run the program for long.

How a week is structured

Four days a week, one main lift per day. Each day has three parts:

  1. Main sets: three sets of the day’s lift, using percentages of a training max. The last set is done for as many good reps as you can.
  2. BBB sets: five sets of ten at a moderate weight, about 50% to 60% of the training max.
  3. Assistance: a little pushing, pulling, single-leg and core work. Keep it short.

The four-week wave

The main sets follow a repeating wave. Percentages are of the training max, not your true max.

WeekSet 1Set 2Set 3
1 (fives)65% × 575% × 585% × 5+
2 (threes)70% × 380% × 390% × 3+
3 (5/3/1)75% × 585% × 395% × 1+
4 (deload)40% × 550% × 560% × 5

After each cycle, raise the training max by 5 lb (about 2.5 kg) for bench and press, and 10 lb (about 5 kg) for squat and deadlift. That’s it. Small, boring, and it keeps working for a long time.

Setting the training max

The training max is a deliberately low number, usually 85% to 90% of your one-rep max. Every percentage comes from it, so the weights feel manageable even in the 1+ week. If you are unsure, start lower. Wendler’s rule of thumb is that it is better to start too light than too heavy.

If the 5+, 3+ or 1+ set ever gives you fewer reps than written, your training max is too high. Lower it by about 10% and build back up.

Assistance work

BBB already adds a lot of volume. Keep assistance short: something for upper back (rows, pull-ups, face pulls), a single-leg exercise, and some core work. A total of 25 to 50 reps per category is plenty for most people.

A sample first cycle

Weights are rounded to loadable numbers. The plus set might go for 8 to 10 reps in the first cycle. That’s expected: the training max is meant to be conservative.

Running it at home

5/3/1 BBB needs a barbell, rack and bench for the main lifts and BBB sets. Assistance work is flexible: dumbbell rows, pull-ups, split squats and hanging leg raises all fit. If your plates are limited, round the percentages down to the closest weight you can load and accept a rep or two more on the plus set. Our plate calculator shows what you can build.

BBB vs other 5/3/1 templates

5/3/1 has many templates. BBB is the volume-heavy one. Others keep the same main sets but change what follows them: lighter supplemental work, heavier sets at the first-set percentage, or more conditioning. If 5×10 leaves you too sore to recover, a lighter template keeps the same main-lift progress with less fatigue.

Common mistakes

  1. Training max too high. The most common mistake by far. It turns a sustainable program into a grind.
  2. Going too heavy on BBB sets. They should be challenging by the last set, not maximal. Start at 50%.
  3. Chasing huge plus sets every week. Push them hard but with clean form. A few more reps is a good week; a grinder is not.
  4. Piling on assistance. More is not better here. Recovery is what lets the training max keep rising.

Who it suits

BBB suits intermediate lifters who want size and strength, can train four days a week, and like a plan that does not change much. If you want faster weekly progress and can recover from it, a linear program like nSuns moves quicker. If you are past linear progress, BBB is a reliable next step.

Frequently asked questions

What training max should I use for 5/3/1?

Wendler’s original books use 90% of your one-rep max. His later books suggest 85% for most lifters. Lower is safer: you lose nothing by starting light, and the training max rises every cycle.

Should BBB sets be the same lift or the opposite lift?

The original version uses the same lift. Some later versions pair opposite lifts, such as squat main sets with deadlift BBB sets. Same-lift is simpler and builds the most practice.

Is 5/3/1 BBB good for building muscle?

Yes. The 5×10 sets add a lot of moderate-weight volume, which is why BBB is one of the most popular 5/3/1 templates for size.

Do I need the deload week?

Wendler’s later books make deloads more flexible, often after two cycles. If you feel beaten up, take it. If you feel fresh, follow the version of the template you are running.

Where can I read the full program?

Jim Wendler’s books, including 5/3/1 Forever, are the source. This guide explains the structure in our own words; the books cover far more detail, variations and the reasoning behind them.
