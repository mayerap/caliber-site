GZCLP explained: T1, T2, T3 and a free template

GZCLP is a beginner-to-intermediate strength program that pairs one heavy lift, one moderate lift and one light accessory every workout. When you stall, it changes the sets and reps before it lowers the weight.

What GZCLP is

GZCLP is a free linear progression program by powerlifter and coach Cody Lefever, who posts as u/gzcl on Reddit. It is built on his GZCL method, which organizes training into three tiers of heavy, moderate and light work. GZCLP applies that method to lifters who can still add weight to the bar most sessions. Discussion and the creator’s notes live on r/gzcl.

The three tiers

  • T1, heavy: one main lift for 5 sets of 3. The last set is “3+”, meaning as many reps as you can with good form.
  • T2, moderate: a second main lift for 3 sets of 10.
  • T3, light: an accessory for 3 sets of 15. The last set is “15+”, done for as many reps as you can.

A useful rule from the GZCL method is 1:2:3. For every rep of T1, aim for about two reps of T2 and three of T3. The heavy work builds strength. The lighter work builds the muscle and practice that feed it.

The weekly rotation

There are four workouts. The T1 and T2 lifts swap places, so each main lift is trained heavy one day and moderate another.

A1SquatBench pressLat pulldown
B1Overhead pressDeadliftDumbbell row
A2Bench pressSquatLat pulldown
B2DeadliftOverhead pressDumbbell row

Run the four workouts in order, 3 or 4 days a week. On 3 days, the rotation simply carries over into the next week.

Choosing starting weights

  • T1: start around 85% of your 5-rep max. The first few weeks should feel easy. That is the point.
  • T2: start with a weight you could lift for about 15 reps, so 3 sets of 10 feel smooth.
  • T3: start light enough that the 15+ set ends well past 15 reps.

Starting T1 too heavy is the most common GZCLP mistake. If the first session feels hard, the starting weight is wrong, not you.

Progression and the stall stages

When you complete every rep, add weight next time: about 5 lb (2.5 kg) for bench and overhead press, and about 10 lb (5 kg) for squat and deadlift. For T3, add weight once the last set reaches 25 reps.

When you miss reps, GZCLP changes the scheme instead of the weight:

TierStage 1Stage 2Stage 3After stage 3
T15×3+6×2+10×1+Test a new 5-rep max, restart at about 85% of it
T23×103×83×6Restart at 3×10 slightly heavier than your last stage 1 weight

The stages keep total reps roughly the same while the weight keeps rising. They buy you weeks or months of extra progress before a reset.

Rest times

Rest about 3 to 5 minutes between T1 sets, 2 to 3 minutes for T2, and 60 to 90 seconds for T3. Short T3 rest keeps sessions under an hour.

Common mistakes

  1. Starting too heavy on T1. You lose the easy early weeks that build momentum.
  2. Skipping T3. The high-rep work is a big part of why the program works. Don’t cut it to save time.
  3. Sloppy AMRAP sets. Stop the 3+ and 15+ sets when form breaks, not when you physically can’t move.
  4. Changing the rotation. Swapping T1 and T2 lifts on a whim breaks the balance of heavy and moderate work.

What comes after GZCLP

When the stall stages stop working, you have moved past linear progression. Cody Lefever has follow-up programs, including The Rippler and General Gainz, or you can move to any program with planned blocks. See what to run after your program ends.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between GZCL and GZCLP?

GZCL is Cody Lefever’s overall method: the tier system and the idea of balancing heavy, moderate and light work. GZCLP is one specific program built on that method, a linear progression for lifters near the end of their beginner phase.

Is GZCLP 3 or 4 days a week?

It is four workouts that rotate. You can run them 3 days a week, so each week starts where the last left off, or 4 days a week to hit every workout weekly. Three days is the common starting point.

What happens when I stall on GZCLP?

You change the set and rep scheme instead of the weight. T1 moves from 5×3 to 6×2 to 10×1, and T2 moves from 3×10 to 3×8 to 3×6. When the last stage stalls, retest and restart that lift with a lighter weight.

Can I change the T3 accessories?

Yes. T3 is the most flexible tier. Pick exercises for weak points or muscles the main lifts miss, like upper back, rear delts or arms. Keep the reps high and the rest short.

How long should I run GZCLP?

Until the stall stages stop producing progress, which for most lifters is several months. Then move to a program with planned blocks, such as GZCL’s own intermediate programs or a periodized program.

Can I run GZCLP at home?

Yes, if you have a barbell, rack and bench for the T1 and T2 lifts. T3 lifts can use dumbbells, bands or a pull-up bar.
