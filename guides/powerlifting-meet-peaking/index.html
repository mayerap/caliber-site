How to peak for a powerlifting meet

Peaking means cutting training volume over the final weeks while keeping weights heavy, so fatigue clears and your strength shows on meet day. Most lifters finish heavy squats and deadlifts 7 to 10 days out and do a last light session a few days before.

The idea behind a peak

Peaking means arranging training so you are at your strongest on one specific day. Over the final weeks, volume comes down, intensity stays high, and fatigue clears so the strength you have built shows up on the platform.

What strong lifters actually do

Two studies describe real tapering practice well:

  • Elite New Zealand raw powerlifters (Pritchard and colleagues, 2016): total volume peaked about 5 weeks out and average intensity about 2 weeks out. During the taper, volume dropped by roughly 59% while intensity was kept about the same. The last session was about 3.7 days before the meet, accessories were removed about two weeks out, and lifters said deadlifts took longest to recover from.
  • A survey of 364 American and Canadian raw powerlifters (reviewed by Stronger By Science): most did their final heavy squat and deadlift 7 to 10 days out and their final heavy bench within 7 days. Tapers generally lasted under 10 days.

The common thread: cut volume hard, keep the weights heavy-ish, finish heavy work about a week out, and don’t take too long off.

A simple 10-week peak

Weeks outFocusMain lifts
10 to 6BuildMore sets of 4 to 6 at RPE 7 to 8; normal accessories
5 to 3IntensifyFewer sets, triples and doubles at RPE 8 to 9; trim accessories
2PeakHeavy singles around your opener and second attempt; remove most accessories
1 (meet week)TaperA few light, fast sets early in the week; last session 3 to 5 days out

Shorten the early phases if you have less time. The last two weeks are the part to protect.

Choosing attempts

  • Opener: about 90% of your expected best. You should be able to make it on a bad day.
  • Second: about 95% to 97%. A solid lift, often a small PR.
  • Third: your goal, based on how the second moved.

Plan attempts in your local federation’s units. Kilo meets only allow certain increments, so round your plan before meet day.

Meet week

  1. Keep sessions short. Practice the commands and your setup at light weights.
  2. Sleep and eat normally. Don’t try new foods or supplements.
  3. Pack early: belt, sleeves, shoes, singlet, chalk, food, water and your attempt plan.
  4. Plan warm-ups so your last warm-up finishes a few minutes before your opener.

Planning meet-day warm-ups

Warm-ups on meet day should look like your training warm-ups, just timed to the flight. A typical squat warm-up for a 450 lb opener might be the bar, then 135, 225, 315, 375 and 415, with the last warm-up about 90% of the opener. Count the lifters ahead of you so your last warm-up finishes 5 to 8 minutes before your attempt.

If you are making weight

If your weight class requires a cut, plan it alongside your peak, not after it, and check your federation’s weigh-in timing first. A short window between weigh-in and lifting leaves little time to recover from a big cut. For a first meet, the simplest choice is to lift in the class you are already in.

Common mistakes

  1. Testing maxes in the final two weeks. Heavy singles near your opener are enough.
  2. Taking a full week off. You lose the groove. Keep light training going.
  3. An ambitious opener. Missing your opener puts the whole meet at risk.
  4. Changing technique late. Peak with the technique you have.

Frequently asked questions

How far out should I start peaking?

Most peaks run 6 to 12 weeks, with the heaviest work in the last two to three weeks and a short taper at the end. Elite New Zealand powerlifters studied by Pritchard and colleagues peaked volume about five weeks out and intensity about two weeks out.

When should my last heavy squat and deadlift be?

A large survey of North American raw powerlifters found most did their last heavy squat and deadlift 7 to 10 days out, and their last heavy bench within a week. Deadlift usually needs the most recovery time.

How should I pick my opener?

Pick a weight you could lift on your worst day: about 90% of your expected best, and ideally a weight you have hit for a triple in training.

Should I stop training entirely before the meet?

Not for long. The elite lifters in the Pritchard study did their final session about four days out and reported that taking a week or more off tended to backfire.

Do I need a coach to peak?

No, but a plan matters. Decide your attempts, taper and warm-ups before meet week, so meet day has no decisions left.
