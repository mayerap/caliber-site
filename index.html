    How the perpetual program works

    No finish line, no program hopping. Each block is built from what the last one taught.

    1. Build

      The block opens with volume weeks to add work capacity and muscle.

    2. Push

      Volume comes down and loads go up as you get closer to your limits.

    3. Recover

      A lighter week lets fatigue clear so the strength you built shows up.

    4. Repeat, stronger

      Your next block starts automatically from your new numbers.

    Questions

    How do I try the beta?

    Open the beta link on your iPhone. It installs Apple’s free TestFlight app, then Caliber. The beta is free, and your training carries over to the App Store version at launch.

    Which spreadsheets can I import?

    Excel and CSV files, including programs laid out week by week, which is how most popular programs are built. If a column doesn’t read cleanly, you can fix it before your first workout.

    Do I need my own program?

    No. Tell Caliber how many days you train and what you’re training for, and it builds a perpetual program for you.

    Can I train at the gym and at home on the same program?

    Yes. Tell Caliber what equipment you have at home. Pick gym or home when you train, or set a default for certain sessions or weekdays, and Caliber swaps any exercise you can’t do there for one that trains the same muscles. Your progression keeps going either way.

    What does “perpetual” mean?

    Your training runs in six-week blocks. When one ends, the next one starts automatically from your updated numbers, so you never finish a program and wonder what to do next.

    Do I need an Apple Watch?

    No. Everything works on iPhone. With a Watch you can log sets from your wrist and see live heart rate during cardio.

    Is there an Android version?

    Caliber launches on iPhone first. Android is planned.

    How much will it cost?

    The beta is free. At launch there’s a free trial, then a single subscription. People on the launch list hear the price first.
